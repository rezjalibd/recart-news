هر تراک از «Grand Theft Auto VI: The Album» رتبه‌بندی شد

منبع: Billboard • ۲۹ آبان • ۲۱:۱۷ • ۱۸ دقیقه مطالعه
لحظات تک‌فرهنگی در جامعه در اواسط دهه 2020 بسیار نادر شده‌اند، اما ورود Grand Theft Auto VI به همان اندازه که در این دهه شاهد بودیم، یک موج جزر و مدی یکپارچه با فرهنگ پاپ را ایجاد کرد. Rockstar Games، Grand Theft Auto VI: The Album را در روز جمعه (20 نوامبر) از طریق Atl...

Monocultural moments in society have become extremely rare in the mid-2020s, but the arrival of Grand Theft Auto VI has brought about as strong a unified pop-culture tidal wave as we’ve seen this decade.

Rockstar Games released the Grand Theft Auto VI: The Album on Friday (Nov. 20) through Atlantic Records as an accompanying soundtrack to the long-awaited video game, which arrived 24 hours earlier.

Rockstar didn’t spare any expense when piecing together the 34-track album, which features Playboi Carti, Dua Lipa, Cardi B, Lana Del Rey, Gunna, Don Toliver, Kodak Black, Yung Lean, Future, Metro Boomin, Travis Scott, Fred again.., PinkPantheress, Morgan Wallen, Tame Impala, Fuerza Regida and more.

GTA VI will look to make even more history with a No. 1 debut on the charts — as no video game soundtrack has topped the Billboard 200 before.

Artist-hosted radio stations dominate the Vice City airwaves, with stations helmed or curated by the likes of Bad Bunny, Lana Del Rey, Morgan Wallen, Trina and Trick Daddy, PinkPantheress, Burna Boy and Robyn.

The Grand Theft Auto VI festivities will continue into the weekend, with a music festival at Miami’s Mana Wynwood on Nov. 21. The festival is set to have Cardi B, Travis Scott, Rauw Alejandro and more hit the stage, including a Cactus Jack ensemble featuring Scott, Don Toliver, SoFaygo and a cameo from Florida’s own Kodak Black.

Take a break from perusing Leonida with Jason and Lucia and find all 34 tracks from Grand Theft Auto: The Album ranked in order below.

Who knew Omar Apollo had a funky little party-starter like this up his sleeve? Drawing on the iconic Stalag riddim immortalized by Sister Nancy’s “Bam Bam,” “No Me Busques” bursts with a wailing chorus with an infectious call-and-response hook. Oh, and that festive saxophone riff. Lyrically, there’s not much to it beyond Apollo’s repeated insistence that someone stop looking for him. The groove does most of the talking. — ISABELA RAYGOZA

This track is a little different from what I’m used to, but I like it. It plays during the opening credits of GTA VI and serves as the opening track on the game’s album. Sonically, it has a heavy ’80s-inspired synth pulse, electro-disco drums and electric piano that make me feel like it could fit perfectly into any season of Stranger Things .

It’s the perfect way to open the game’s soundtrack. The song is about starting from zero and hustling your way toward success, which feels right at home in the world of GTA . It sets the tone for the kind of journey players are about to embark on. — CHRISTOPHER CLAXTON

Primal percussion, glitchy electronics and unbridled sexual energy collide on Arca’s self-produced “Sandunguea.” The Venezuelan experimentalist delivers a delirious invitation to dance and indulge, peppering her verses with colorful slang from her homeland. Against abrasive beats, her vocals switch between flirtation and feverish demands, breathy moans and fragmented chants. “ Nuestra pasarela es la calle ” (the street is our runway), she declares. In true Arca fashion, the dance floor becomes a space for uninhibited self-expression, where pleasure and chaos go hand in hand. — I.R.

“All Again” is about missing someone after a relationship has ended, and wondering whether the love, memories and heartbreak were worth it in the end. It captures that feeling of looking back on a relationship and questioning whether you’d put yourself through it all over again. I’m not sure I would. Would you? — C.C.

Rina Sawayama made waves with her assist on Charli xcx’s “Beg for You” in 2022, and the Japanese pop artist/model is back in the mainstream with the brooding “TRDWFE.” I’m going to be hearing Sawayama asking us to “submit” repeatedly when my head hits the pillow tonight — although “TRDWFE” sounds like it was tailor-made more for the likes of HBO’s The Idol than the GTA vibe. — MICHAEL SAPONARA

Metro is back on production for this one. It’s a quick track about flexing, so you already know the deal: money, cars, clothes and relationships. Thug keeps it short and straight to the point, delivering the kind of flex-heavy record that fits right into the GTA lifestyle. Just remember, anything born out of a criminal lifestyle comes with real consequences. — C.C.

Fcukers is back. The NYC duo’s first release since Ö comes with the house-leaning “Only One” and they’re here to start the party and blow out your speakers. If there’s a rave somewhere in Leonida, Fcukers is headlining. Can we get Tinashe to sprinkle her Popstar magic on a remix? Vocalist Shanny Wise even moonlights as a co-host of GTA Radio’s Stockyard FM alongside PinkPantheress and The Dare in the video game. — M.S.

All the baddies to the front, please! “GMA” stands for “Get Money Anthem,” because if you can’t make money in GTA , what the hell can you do? Hope y’all caught that reference. This is an upbeat party anthem celebrating women who make their own money, no matter how they earn it. JT isn’t knocking anybody’s hustle, and at this point, neither am I. Whether you’re stacking cash in the game or in real life, the message is to stay focused on your goals and tune out the criticism. Shoutout to all the ladies working traditional 9-to-5s, earning college degrees, dancing or hustling on the side. We see you! — C.C.

Keith Richards puts his own spin on Jimmy Reed’s 1961 blues classic “Bright Lights, Big City” for Grand Theft Auto VI: The Album. His version takes a different approach from Reed’s original, but Keith sounds right at home here. It’s a fresh take on a blues classic, and one that fits perfectly into the world of GTA . — C.C.

With GTA returning to Vice City, having Kodak Black involved was a must. He’s even a character playing himself in the video game’s campaign. A Yak and Don Toliver collaboration seems like an odd pairing on its face — then you mix in the chaotic skittering production, and both the artists and listeners are playing catch-up for the duration of “Let’s Rock.” — M.S.

Tee Grizzley is not one to mess with. “IKYFL” is an unapologetic statement about loyalty, street credibility, success and refusing to let anyone question how far he’s come. The track captures Tee’s mentality of protecting his circle, enjoying the rewards of his success and staying ready for anyone who tries to challenge him. This is one of those tracks that makes you feel untouchable when you’re cruising through Vice City. — C.C.

Tove Lo wasn’t a name I expected to see on the tracklist for GTA VI, but she makes it work and shuts down any detractors who didn’t think she’d belong. “Blood in Your Eyes” is the blaring experimental pop that should be pumping the dance floor at Leonida’s version of the Space and E11EVEN nightclubs in the game. — M.S.

Morgan Wallen colors outside the lines of country while leaning on his pop and R&B influences for “Last Thing You Need.” It’s a smooth, laid-back track for fans to throw on for a late-night cruise through the swamp lands of GTA ‘s Leonida. — M.S.

Just when you think you’ve settled into the sleek reggaetón groove of “X,” JHAYCO throws a dance-floor curveball. Co-produced by the Puerto Rican star alongside Tainy and COZY, the track finds Jhay assuring a love interest that he’s nothing like her ex, dishing out a stream of suggestive compliments. But the real thrill comes during the bridge, when bright synths and a pulsating electronic beat give way to a euphoric break before the dembow kicks back in. It’s a catchy little club banger that doubles as a reminder of Jhay’s knack for melding reggaetón with EDM. — I.R.

Of course, the GTA team got the legend Robert Glasper to help close out the album! Bringing together bassist Stanley Clarke, drummer Yussef Dayes and pianist Robert Glasper, this track is straight vibes. No lyrics needed, just let the musicians do their thing. Named after Miami’s historic Overtown neighborhood, the track connects the game’s Vice City map to the real-world roots of Miami. A smooth way to bring the album home. — C.C.

This is the perfect song to play after a mission doesn’t go as planned. Burna is reminding us that no matter the setback or how people treat you, you’re still unstoppable. Nobody is better than you, so keep pushing and get “bigger, bigger, bigger, bigger, hey!” — C.C.

This is baby-making music. Dâm-Funk and The Time’s Jesse Johnson supply the groovy, funky vibes that have been a staple of the GTA series for over two decades. I’m glad the soundtrack composers made room for them here, because Gen Z isn’t going to know what hit them when a new generation of gamers presses play. — M.S.

JENNIE is taking another “Power Trip” with this dance-pop track. In JENNIE’s city, there are no limits. This is a celebration of confidence, status and the freedom to do whatever you want without worrying about who approves. She embraces a larger-than-life persona, putting herself at the center of attention while making it clear that she knows her worth. But there’s nothing wrong with a little self-love, so is this really a power trip? — C.C.

If you see Future and Metro Boomin on a track, nine times out of 10, it’s going to be a banger, and this song proves just that. Shoutout to Yung Lean, because I like the track, but I’m not entirely sure if his presence fits. We have Metro handling the production, of course, and Future’s trap-influenced sound just works perfectly over Metro’s production. The two have such a natural chemistry, and this track is another example of why their collaborations rarely miss. – C.C.

Zara Larsson is chasing carefree desire with “Fast Emotion,” and she has no shame about it. She just wants you to “make it reckless, leave [her] breathless, make [her] heart race.” This track is bright, upbeat and full of energy, making it perfect for cruising down Vice City. “Fast Emotion” is all about craving an intense, spontaneous connection and allowing yourself to get swept up in the excitement of the moment. Through imagery of summer nights, physical attraction and speed, Zara captures a romance that feels exhilarating. — C.C.

Outside of Drake, no rapper made more of 2026 than Don Toliver. “TF?” provides the gloomy soundscape where Toliver is at home, compared to “Let’s Rock.” The raunchy tune could’ve been left on the cutting room floor during his OCTANE sessions, but is a worthy addition to the GTA album. — M.S.

A Cherokee loaded with contraband, police sirens blaring and a desperate escape through the city — “Macacoa 2000” opens like a scene straight out of Grand Theft Auto . Over hard-hitting reggaetón produced by Dímelo Ninow, reminiscent of the genre’s gritty 2000s heyday, Rauw Alejandro narrates a chaotic cop chase in a rapid-fire flow. Near the end, the track switches gears into sultrier territory, with the silky R&B vocals that marked his breakthrough. But beneath the bravado is a deeper reverence for reggaetón’s roots. At the beginning, he salutes the genre’s pioneers and reflects on his own longevity: “ Se trata de trascender en la música ” (It’s about transcending through music). — I.R.

Lana Del Rey shines as Delta Dawne in her role as a co-host of the rebellious Back Country Radio station in GTA . It’s only right she also delivered on the soundtrack, as LDR revels in a tragic romance for her “Crazy for You” ballad. Enter a Jason and Lucia romance scene from the campaign, with Lana providing the accompaniment. — M.S.

Fuerza Regida trades corridos for a breezy reggaetón groove on “Suzuki,” a flirtatious, raunchy cut that finds the San Bernardino hitmakers venturing into new territory. Produced by Synthetic, the track glides on glossy electronic textures and a laid-back dembow bounce, while frontman Jesús Ortiz Paz delivers a string of shameless come-ons. From pole dancing and risqué fantasies to a cheeky reference to tusi (a.k.a. pink cocaine), the lyrics leave little to the imagination. The beat may be easygoing, but JOP clearly has no intention of behaving himself. — I.R.

If half of Camila Cabello’s heart is in Havana, the other half lies in Miami. The dulcet “Sweet Like Candy” is a solid effort from CC — and like a good piece of candy, it’s not too heavy or sour, as the Fifth Harmony solo star sticks the landing with a fun, surface-level pop song. It also makes me think about where she has been in the last two years, with Cabello largely out of the pop scene since 2024’s C,XOXO . Time for a comeback, Camila. — M.S.

“Everybody getting’ loose!” Produced by Guy-Manuel de Homem-Christo, Travis definitely found his pocket on this one. I can see this track growing in popularity over time, and it’s already one of my favorites on the album. You have to crank the volume all the way up for this one. There’s so much electronic-infused chaos packed into the production, making it feel like hip-hop built for the club. It fits flawlessly into the GTA universe. — C.C.

Gunna turned back the clock to the days long before he was running 5Ks and flexed a six-pack as a gym rat. The Atlanta native reunites with frequent collaborator Turbo on the production side for a hard-hitting trap banger that boasts a flow that will sound familiar to early Gunna fans from the days of his Drip or Drown and Drip Season series, when he was still rising through the rap ranks. — M.S.

For all the tenderness with which Kali Uchis and Helado Negro sing to each other, their message couldn’t be more blunt: lárgate (or “f–k off”). The Colombian American singer and Ecuadorian American musician explore the emotional contradictions of a relationship that’s run its course, alternating between wistful reflections and outright dismissal. The sweetness of their vocal interplay almost belies the bitterness of their words. — I.R.

Cardi B will always pop her s—t, and “Don’t Chart” is no different. From the Bronx all the way to Vice City, Cardi is talking her talk! The track marks her second release of 2026, following the arrival of “AH HA” in July. This is Cardi through and through: she’s “still poppin’, still winnin’, [and] you still hatin’.” The bouncy production is packed with flex after flex, touching on confidence, wealth, chart success and designer labels. Thank you, Cardi, for another boastful tongue-in-cheek anthem. — C.C.

I imagine Playboi Carti has thousands of songs in a folder on hard drives that he could lend out when needed. Whether it was Carti himself or right-hand producer F1LTHY, they made the right choice going with “Ahh Yea.” For Carti’s standards, “Ahh Yea” leans more into the poppier side of his artistry, and could easily be seen playing on the GTA radio while gamers cruise around Leonida. “Ahh Yea” seems like a holdover from the MUSIC era, and I wish we got more loosies like this from King Vamp. — M.S.

Argentine duo CA7RIEL & Paco Amoroso bring their irreverence to the dance floor on “Sexy Magic,” a bouncy, seductive electronic collaboration with British pop artist PinkPantheress, producer Fred again.. and French house veteran Étienne de Crécy. Riding a groovy bassline and shimmering synths, the track pairs Paco’s playful Spanish delivery with PinkPantheress’ breathy English vocals. Psychedelic around the edges and funky at its core, “Sexy Magic” sounds tailor-made for a neon-lit night cruising through Vice City. — I.R.

New York is in the building! That opening line in the first verse, “You look prettier in person,” hits in this social media era. We all know what it’s like to meet someone in person after finding them online and being happy that they actually look like their pictures — or, in this case, look even better. Boogie, you did your thing with this one, my boy. This is about to go platinum in the crib: “I don’t wanna fight, I wanna dance tonight” — a perfect track for a late-night drive through Vice City. — C.C.

Dua Lipa has proven to be the queen of a soundtrack when she dished out the top 10 Hot 100 bop “Dance the Night” for Barbie in 2023. With an intro that seems to draw from Phil Collins’ “Don’t Lose My Number” and later interpolates KISS’ “I Was Made for Lovin’ You,” the pop star has a penchant for taking songs of yesteryear and making them digestible for today’s audiences. “Go Easy” is no different for the hitmaker, as Dua clears the decks and gears up to launch a new era in the first quarter of ’27. — M.S.

“Flower in a Hurricane” is the preeminent song from GTA VI . In a short time, it’s already essentially become the video game’s theme song, providing the soundtrack to key scenes, clips and compilations going viral on social media. Justice meshes its EDM roots with King Gizzard & the Lizard Wizard’s rock while SZA and Kevin Parker join forces to sing about surviving the storm to make it out alive on the other side. It perfectly balances the angst of everything falling apart at any moment on the journey to Vice City glory. — M.S.
