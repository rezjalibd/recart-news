Valve می گوید ممکن است مجبور شود قیمت ماشین های بخار با محدودیت عرضه را افزایش دهد

منبع: Ars Technica • ۲۹ آبان • ۱۹:۴۱ • ۲ دقیقه مطالعه
هنگامی که Steam Machine برای اولین بار در ژوئن شروع به حمل و نقل کرد، Valve گفت که واحدهای کمتری را با قیمتی بالاتر از آنچه در ابتدا برنامه ریزی شده بود تولید می کند، زیرا نمی تواند منابع کمیاب RAM و قطعات ذخیره سازی را با هزینه معقول تامین کند. اکنون، در حالی که آخرین ...

Text settings Story text Size Small Standard Large Width * Standard Wide Links Standard Orange * Subscribers only Learn more Minimize to nav When the Steam Machine first started shipping in June , Valve said it was producing fewer units at a higher price than initially planned because it couldn’t secure supplies of scarce RAM and storage components at a reasonable cost. Now, as the last of the initial batch of Steam Machine reservations are finally being fulfilled, Valve says those supply and cost issues will continue into the foreseeable future and may even require a price change.

“We are continuing to build more Steam Machine units, but memory and storage costs continue to increase at unprecedented levels,” Valve wrote in a blog post on Thursday . “As a result we don’t yet know the number we are able to make, and at what price we’ll be able to offer them. The 2TB model in particular will be especially constrained.”

If Valve does have to raise the price of the Steam Machine, it will be in good company. All three major console manufacturers instituted historically atypical price increases in 2025 , with additional price hikes happening this year.

For the Steam Machine, though, Valve may be more susceptible to supply-related price shocks than its larger competitors in the gaming hardware space. Valve “likely [doesn’t] have the same kind of priority as a Nintendo, Sony, or Microsoft when it comes to suppliers,” F-Squared analyst Michael Futter told us earlier this year . And while the bigger players can still negotiate somewhat with chip suppliers, Valve’s Steam Machine is “a niche product that cannot benefit in the same way when it comes to procurement,” Niko Partners analyst Daniel Ahmad said.

While Valve has not publicly discussed total Steam Machine sales, estimates based on public Steam Top Sellers charts suggest the company was selling 12,000 to 15,000 a week back in July. By contrast, the PS5 has averaged over 300,000 weekly unit sales worldwide in its roughly six years on the market.

For now, at least, Valve says it has fulfilled an expectation it set in July that “everyone in the reservation queue will have the option to purchase by the end of this year.” The company now says there is “no waitlist remaining” for the 512GB Steam Machine model and that “purchase invites” have been sent to the remaining randomized 2TB reservation holders (who placed orders back in April). The 512GB model was even briefly back in stock on the Steam Store yesterday, even as Valve continues to pare down the waitlist for 2TB stock.

Valve says it plans to release additional updates on Steam Machine availability in January.
