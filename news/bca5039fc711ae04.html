مگان مورونی و الکس وارن وقتی در ماشین می شنوند آهنگ خوبی می شناسند

منبع: Rolling Stone • ۲۹ آبان • ۲۱:۲۸ • ۵ دقیقه مطالعه
برای دو هنرمند که زندگی آنها حول موسیقی می چرخد، جاده نوع متفاوتی از تجربه شنیداری را ارائه می دهد. برای مگان مورونی، خواننده برتر چارت کانتری و الکس وارن، آهنگساز موسیقی پاپ، لزوماً وقتی استودیو را ترک می‌کند، تمام نمی‌شود. گاهی اوقات نیاز به تست درایو دارد. برای وارن،...

For country chart-topper Megan Moroney and pop hitmaker Alex Warren, a song isn’t necessarily finished when it leaves the studio. Sometimes, it needs a test drive.

For Warren, listening to music in the car holds sentimental meaning. On his new album, Wildchild , he pays tribute to his late father, who died of cancer when Warren was just nine years old. Some of his most powerful musical memories with his dad are inseparable from time spent in the car.

“My only memory of music growing up was getting in the car and listening to my dad’s CDs, and John Mayer was his guy,” Warren said. “When my dad passed away, I got in his car and [Mayer’s album] came on. When I wrote this song for my dad called ‘Wildchild,’ I put his voice on it, and I asked [Mayer] to do a guitar solo on it, so my dad and John have a song.”

The car is also where Warren discovered Moroney’s music. He recalls listening to her breakout hit “Tennessee Orange” in the car with his wife, who knew every word.

“She knows that song like the back of her hand, and it’s really cool because she applies it to her own life,” Warren said of Moroney’s autobiographical debut, which tells the story of a die-hard Georgia football fan falling for a Tennessee Volunteers fan.

“It encapsulates the kind of music my fans like,” said Moroney, whose sharp-eyed songwriting turns specific details into universal feelings that resonate with the masses.

“It’s creative, it’s honest, there’s the hook, and it’s storytelling. I feel like at the root of all of my songs, that’s what it is. And obviously I’m a Georgia Bulldog, so it’s just very me.”

Moroney and Warren may come from different corners of the music world, but the two found plenty of common ground during a conversation outside Fox & Locke, a storied live-music venue tucked into the rolling countryside of Leiper’s Fork, Tennessee. The discussion was part of Rolling Stone ’s Musicians on Musicians series. This time, Lexus sought to connect two artists from different backgrounds in an entirely new way by trading the usual lounge-like venues that host Musicians on Musicians conversations for the lounge-inspired quiet cabin of an all-electric 2027 Lexus TZ, which will make its debut this winter. As the conversation continued, Moroney and Warren learned that their paths to stardom have more than a few parallels. Both got an early boost from TikTok before breaking through with massive singles from their debut albums — Moroney with her five-times-platinum “Tennessee Orange,” Warren with his record-breaking “Ordinary.” Both have crossed genre lines through collaborations, and both released hit albums this year: Moroney’s autobiographical Cloud 9 and Warren’s deeply personal Wildchild . They also share a belief in one essential part of the creative process: the all-important “car test.”

For Warren, that test played a pivotal role in one of the biggest songs of his career. After receiving an early, stripped-down version of “Ordinary” — little more than his voice and a piano — he played it for his wife while they were driving. Her reaction told him everything he needed to know. “She was like, ‘I want more of it!’”

The experience changed the way he shares new music. “Now I only show people songs in my car.”

He might be onto something. Whether you’re a professional musician or more of a carpool-karaoke type, there’s something about hearing a song on the road that changes the experience. A lyric lands a little harder. A melody feels a little bigger. And sometimes, the right song at the right moment can turn a daily drive into something worth remembering. It helps when the sound system is up to the task. The all-electric Lexus TZ features an available 21-speaker Mark Levinson audio system, engineered specifically for the TZ’s three-row cabin to deliver the kind of rich, immersive sound that feels the way it was meant to be heard.

Of course, when your life revolves around music, sometimes the best soundtrack is no soundtrack at all. For artists like Moroney and Warren, who spend much of their time on the road, a long drive can also offer a rare chance to unplug, catch up, and simply have a conversation. The TZ makes room for both experiences, and that balance between sound and silence feels particularly fitting for two artists who know a song’s power to make a personal story resonate far beyond its original inspiration.

It’s a reminder that the songs we carry with us are rarely just about the music. They’re tied to people, places, and moments that stick around long after the last note fades. And sometimes, the best way to know whether a song really works is to get behind the wheel, turn it up, and see where it takes you.
