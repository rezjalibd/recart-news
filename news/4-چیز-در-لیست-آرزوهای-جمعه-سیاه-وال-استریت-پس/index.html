Toby Shepheard/Reuters Black Friday shoppers aren't the only ones with a wish list.Investors have a few big asks, too.

From a calmer Nvidia to an end to the Iran war, here's what could give stocks a break by year-end.Sign up for First Trade , Business Insider's daily markets newsletter.

Heading into Thanksgiving, I did a special edition of First Trade focused on what investors should feel thankful for in markets.Now that the holiday has passed and we're emerging from our post-turkey-and-football haze, it's time to look ahead.

As Black Friday shoppers check off their lists, I've put together a wish list of my own — a few things investors would love to see before the year is out.A break from the Nvidia roller coaster Riding Nvidia to new stock-market highs was fun while it lasted.

Now, thanks in part to its CEO scandal, shares are prone to stomach-churning swings, and investors are discovering the downside of relying so heavily on such a titanic company.Huang's return offered a jolt of confidence , but it didn't resolve larger questions about Nvidia governance or the AI trade overall.

A calmer Nvidia would certainly help soothe investors' nerves, but a market less dependent on Nvidia would be even better.Proof that AI can pay for itself It's still too early to tell if the tech giants at the center of the AI trade can turn hundreds of billions of dollars in spending into returns that justify the cost.

There have been bright spots, but plenty of questions remain.Investors have already started to reward companies seeing the most immediately tangible benefits.

The next wish is for those gains to spread.As more businesses put AI to work, can the technology deliver enough revenue and productivity growth to make the huge investment worthwhile? Some relief on inflation and interest rates Cooling inflation could ease pressure on the Fed to keep tightening and give borrowers a break from elevated costs.

For stocks, the wish is especially relevant to expensive growth companies whose valuations are sensitive to rates.A little relief could also help a wider range of stocks find their footing.

And you know what might help with that? … … An end to the Iran war This is not a political statement.I'm merely an impartial markets journalist who's noticed that basically every chart related to US affordability — crude oil , the 10-year Treasury yield , and even consumer-price inflation itself — has gotten markedly worse since the war started.
